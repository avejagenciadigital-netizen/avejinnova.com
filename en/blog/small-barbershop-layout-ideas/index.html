Small barbershop layout ideas that fit two chairs

Published October 30, 2026 · AVEJ Innova

Practical layout ideas to go from one cramped chair to two stations working at once in a small barbershop.

Barbershop: from 1 cramped chair to 2 stations working at once.

In a small barbershop, going from one chair to two working at the same time can nearly double your daily services on the same rent. Most small shops have the space; it is just badly distributed: the chair in the middle, the waiting area right next to it, and products everywhere.

Measure first and mark what can't move

Take the length and width, and mark the door, electrical outlets, the shampoo bowl and any columns. Those fixed points decide where stations can go without heavy construction.

Line the stations up along one wall

In narrow shops, the layout that frees the most space is putting the stations in a row along one wall, each with its own mirror, and keeping the opposite wall for waiting. The barber can move all the way around the chair, and waiting customers stay out of the way.

As a practical check, each station needs room for the chair to turn fully and for the barber to walk behind it without bumping into the next station.

Get the waiting area out of the way

A bench along the opposite wall takes less space than loose chairs and looks tidier. In very narrow shops, waiting can sit near the entrance, where it also makes the window look busy.

One cabinet per station

A shelf or drawer unit next to each mirror stops barbers from crossing paths to find tools. Anything not used in every cut gets stored, not displayed.

Light each station

A dedicated light above each mirror, on top of the general lighting, improves the work and the photo your client takes on the way out. That photo is free advertising.

Your window matters too

From the street, a shop with two chairs working looks more trustworthy than one with a single chair. If your window shows the stations and your brand, it pulls in people walking by.
